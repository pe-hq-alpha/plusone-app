## The market

People book an event in a rush, excited and often confused, with the details scattered across calls, chats, PDFs and half a dozen apps. Nothing lines up, so there is no clear way to make the right decision, and when something doesn't go to plan, as it often doesn't, they end up paying more than they had to. Planners and planning apps each handle a piece, and the sites with vendor lists only list them, with no way to tell what a price includes or how it compares.

India's weddings alone are estimated at about $130 billion a year (Jefferies, 2024), and birthdays, housewarmings and office events add to that. In the 2025 season alone, 46 lakh weddings were expected to spend ₹6.5 lakh crore in 45 days, about a quarter of it (some ₹1.75 lakh crore) on the services PlusOne compares: catering, event management, decor, photography, music, and light and sound (CAIT, Nov 2025). An estimated 87% of the industry is unorganised, the average wedding hires 14 different vendors, 75–87% of bookings are still made offline, and 53% of wedding budgets have to rise while planning (Kotak Mutual Fund, Nov 2025). Ten years ago home services and travel were just as chaotic, and nobody thought home cleaning could have fixed prices; now it does. Events are one of the few large markets still left like this. Events vary more, across many trades and tiers, so PlusOne standardises them to a range: for each category, the way a mid-range brand and a luxury brand each have their own, the range split into Value, Standard, Premium and Luxury. For every trade in the city PlusOne shows the low end, the typical price and the high end, where each quote sits among them, and, once bookings run through PlusOne, what the customers who booked say. The customer gets a transparent view and makes the choice; PlusOne never picks the cheapest for them. The vision, over the next five to ten years: planning and buying an event in India as predictable as booking a cleaner or a trip.

Demand is never the question: there is always a birthday, a housewarming, a wedding function or an office party being planned. What's missing is one organised place to plan and book it, where every price can be seen and compared. People aren't used to seeing it organised, so they end up trusting several people at once. Even event managers have no proper system for it, and no transparency: they quote one price, and their client never sees what each vendor behind it quoted. Customers end up spending more than they have to. PlusOne is built to change that, for families and for the event managers who want to show their clients a clear, itemised price.

## The gap

Planning an event in India runs across calls, WhatsApp, email and spreadsheets, and there is no single place that holds the whole plan: the budget, the vendors, the messages, the guests and the day itself. There is also no place where quotes can be compared, because no two are written alike and the extra charges arrive later. Directory sites list vendors and pass a customer's enquiry to many of them; planner apps leave the comparing to the customer. PlusOne fills both gaps in one app: the whole plan in one place, and every quote on the same lines.

## Three ways in, one paid

*Find vendors* is open to everyone, with no sign-in and no plan: every listed vendor by trade and city, with their packages on the standard sheet, and vendors on the open web. *The planner* (Basic, or Premium with a person) is the paid, end-to-end app: the plan, the budget, vendors written to and chased, every quote on the same lines. *PlusOne Partner* is where vendors list for a small fee to be found and answer requests.

## The vision, ten years out

Online shopping brought sellers under one roof, home services gave cleaning a fixed price, and travel sites put every hotel and flight in one place. Events never made that move: the sites that exist list vendors and sell advertising, and nobody runs the whole thing from the first idea to the day. PlusOne is built to. What's live today, the planner, comparable quotes, verified vendors and the map, is the way in. The aim is to be India's largest and most trusted name for planning events and weddings, where the money moves from customer to vendor through PlusOne and the PlusOne team does the legwork, coordinating every vendor end to end, at any size: a first birthday or a wedding. Even 1% of India's $130 billion a year in weddings alone moving through PlusOne would be about $1.3 billion in bookings a year. Then branding, marketing and distribution at scale bring more vendors and more customers, and each new event makes the comparison sharper for the next.

## What if someone copies it?

They will. Building is the easy part and copying is easier: online shopping has many apps selling much the same things, and the ones that lead won on execution, quality, trust, brand and distribution, not on the app. That's where PlusOne competes. Execution: every event run the same careful way, from a standard sheet and a run of steps for each kind of event, so the hundredth birthday goes as well as the first. Quality and trust: every vendor checked by a person, one accountable company, one contract and a guarantee on every booking made through PlusOne. The network: verified vendors whose track record builds through PlusOne, and price data from every quote and, in time, every final bill, which makes each city's market range real. And the brand and distribution: a name families and event managers think of first, built city by city. None of that is built in a week or copied in one: it takes years, one city and one kind of event at a time, scaled slowly and done well, and that's the plan.

## Where the money is

Subscriptions and the vendor listing pay for the start. The end goal is the commission: once PlusOne has the brand, a verified vendor network and customers' trust, events are booked and paid through PlusOne, and a disclosed share of each booking is PlusOne's. Every step built now leads there: quotes on the same lines make a booking easy to agree, verified vendors with a track record make it safe, and the final bill read against the quote shows it was kept. A first birthday, a 60th or a team offsite is a booking, not only a wedding, so the commission comes from every size of event.

## Why bookings stay on PlusOne

Once a customer has found a vendor, the WhatsApp button could take the deal outside. These are why it doesn't, the way home-services apps keep their bookings (the plan for booking in the app, after the hackathon):
- **The guarantee covers bookings made through PlusOne only.** PlusOne Assured: the quote is honoured line by line with no surprise charges, and a vendor who doesn't turn up is replaced. Book outside, and there is none.
- **Standard prices, locked at booking.** A verified vendor lists standard packages on the standard sheet; booking through PlusOne locks that price, and a listed vendor agrees not to offer the same package for less outside it. Going direct saves nothing and loses the guarantee.
- **Payment in stages.** An advance, then the balance as each stage is done, through the app: safer for the customer than cash up front, and accountable for the vendor.
- **The record only grows on PlusOne.** Ratings come only from bookings made through PlusOne, measured where they can be (on time, the final bill against the quote), and a vendor's level (New, Trusted, Top) only from events done through it. For a small vendor with no website, that record is their brand.
- **Contact after booking.** Partner vendors are reached through PlusOne until the booking is made; vendors found on the open web keep their WhatsApp and Call buttons, since they aren't on PlusOne yet, and are invited on.

## Unit economics

Measured on production, each step costs a cent or less at list price: reading a quote from an email, a PDF or a photo about $0.011 (18 reads, $0.20), finding and cutting out a hall's stage about $0.009, a two-minute voice note at most $0.006, a 47-second vendor call $0.002 plus its reading, a photo check about $0.006 warm. A whole event, with a few dozen quotes and replies read, emails drafted, searches run and RSVPs read, comes to an estimated $1–2 of model calls, well under a subscription; a real event of the founders', planned through PlusOne, will measure it end to end. The commission is the larger line: at the prices in our evaluation set, a 60th for 120 in Chennai comes to about ₹2 lakh across the hall, catering, decor and photography, so an assumed 8% take rate would be about ₹16,000 an event, against about ₹150 of model cost. The rate is an assumption to test in the pilot, not a decision.

Every fee is disclosed. Fair pricing here means informed and comparable: PlusOne shows each price itemised, beside the others and the market range. It never sets one price for everyone or picks the cheapest bid by default. For now, customers pay vendors directly.

## How it reaches people

One city first, where the founders plan their own family's events: interviews with families, event managers and vendors, real quotations collected and their hidden charges mapped, then a hand-run pilot with paying customers on the events that repeat most (birthdays, engagements, housewarmings). Then the rest of India, then other countries, on the same engine adapted to local vendors, customs and prices.
